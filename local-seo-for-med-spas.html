Why med spas are different

Clients are trusting someone with their face

A first time client is nervous. They want to know who will hold the needle, what training that person has, whether the result will look natural, and what it will cost. They read reviews for whether the injector listened, explained the risks, and did not push extra syringes. Then they look at your photos and your team page before they book.

Each treatment is its own search. Lip filler, laser hair removal, microneedling, chemical peels, and medical weight loss are typed by different people with different worries. Many clinics still list them all on one page. A page for each treatment is how a med spa wins those searches.

A client who trusts their injector comes back for years and brings friends.

Google reads health and beauty claims carefully, and so do clients. Pages that promise to take ten years off read as a sales pitch to both. Plain pages about what a treatment does, who it suits, how long it lasts, and who performs it earn more trust and more bookings.

Where the calls go missing

Six things we find on most med spa profiles

None of them look serious alone. Together they send new clients to the clinic down the road.

  1. Day spa as the main categoryThe listing says Day spa or Beauty salon, so Google shows it to people looking for a massage. Medical spa is the primary for a clinic doing injectables and lasers. Every other category has to match real treatments.
  2. One page called TreatmentsInjectables, lasers, facials, peels, and weight loss all on one page. Google ranks whole pages, so a page about everything ranks for nothing.
  3. No word about who does the workClients want to know who injects and what their license is. A site that hides the team behind stock models makes them wonder why.
  4. A business name stuffed with treatmentsAdding Botox and Filler to the name on the profile breaks Google's rules. It can work for a while, and then the profile gets suspended or a competitor reports it.
  5. Stock photos of modelsClients want to see your rooms, your team, and your front desk. Google uses real photos to understand the business, and a stock face tells it nothing.
  6. Review replies that say too muchA reply that mentions someone's treatment confirms they are a client. Short, warm replies with no details are safer and read better to the next person.

Questions

What med spas ask us

Can we show before and after photos?

On your own site, with written consent and an honest note on what a typical result looks like, yes. We keep identifying details out, and your medical team approves every one. Google has its own rules for photos on the profile, and we follow them.

How long until we see new clients?

Usually three to six months to start seeing big results. Fixing the profile and listings moves the map first. Treatment pages and reviews take longer and keep building.

Should our injectors each have a Google profile?

Sometimes. Google allows one for a provider who sees clients, with rules for how it is named. Set up right it can help. Set up wrong it competes with the clinic. We decide that with you.

Can we rank for one treatment we want to grow?

Yes, if you offer it. A category where one exists, a page written for that treatment, real photos, and reviews that mention it tell Google you do it well. We put more of the work into the treatment you want to grow.

Do you put our prices on the site?

Only if you want them there. Some clinics show a starting price per unit or per session, and some explain what drives the cost. Both answer what people are typing, and you decide.

Can we compete with the big med spa chains?

On the map, yes. A chain location is one profile like yours, and Google ranks the closest, best reviewed, best described clinic. You win your own neighborhoods and the treatment searches the chains do not cover well.

Do you write the medical content?

We write treatment pages and city pages in plain words. Your medical director approves anything clinical before it publishes, and we make no claims about outcomes.
