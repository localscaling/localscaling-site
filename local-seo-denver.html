Why Denver is different

One city ringed by a dozen suburbs

Denver is a city and a county at once, and the suburbs around it sit in several other counties. Aurora to the east is a big city of its own. Lakewood and Arvada run west toward the foothills, Westminster and Thornton spread north, and Littleton, Centennial, and Highlands Ranch fill in the south side.

On the map it reads as one metro. In the search box it is a list of town names.

Inside the city, the neighborhoods carry their own names. Someone in Highland types Highland, and a family near Washington Park types Wash Park. A page that only says Denver gives either of them little reason to think you work on their street.

Distance matters more than it looks. Traffic on I-25, I-70, and C-470 makes a short trip on the map feel long, so most people pick someone on their side of town. A law office in Cherry Creek rarely shows on the map for a client in Arvada, however strong the profile.

Two spots on one page

The map gets the call. The listing under it closes the deal.

When someone in Denver or a nearby suburb searches for what you do, Google shows the map first and the regular results under it, and both change with the place they are searching from. A business that holds a spot in both, for that place, gets the call.

We work both. Your Google Business Profile wins the map. Your website wins the listing under it. The audit checks where you stand in each.

Questions

What Denver owners ask us

You are not in Denver. Does that matter?

No. Google looks at your location, your profile, your reviews, and your site, and none of those depend on where we work. We run Denver campaigns remotely and report to you every month.

How long until I see results?

Usually three to six months to start seeing big results. Some neighborhoods and suburbs move faster than others, and we tell you which before you commit.

We are in Lakewood. Should our pages say Lakewood or Denver?

Both, each on its own page. People nearby search for Lakewood, and people further out search for Denver. Your profile keeps your real address, and your site gets a page for each name you want to win.

Can one profile cover the whole metro?

No single listing reaches from Thornton to Highlands Ranch and out to Aurora. You win the area around your address first, then reach further with neighborhood and suburb pages as the profile gets stronger. A second listing needs a real staffed office, and a virtual office or a mailbox can get the whole profile suspended.

How many reviews do we need?

There is no set number. In each area you need more recent reviews than the businesses showing above you, from people who live there, and a steady flow does more than a burst.

We also work in Boulder and the mountain towns. Can you help there?

Yes, with pages for each town you really serve. On the map, distance from your address still counts, so the areas closest to you come first and the far ones lean on your pages and reviews.
