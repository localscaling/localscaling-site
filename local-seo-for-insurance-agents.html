Why insurance is different

People shop for insurance when their life changes

Most people do not think about insurance until they have to. A new house, a new car, a teenager with a new license, a wedding, a baby, a new business. Each of those sends someone to a search bar, often with a deadline attached.

The other big trigger is a renewal notice with a higher number on it. That person is in no rush. They read pages, compare a few agencies, and look through reviews for signs that someone will answer the phone when they have a claim.

A national carrier can outspend you on ads. It cannot sit on every street the way the map needs.

Every line is its own search. Home, auto, life, health, Medicare, and commercial are typed by different people, and a page called insurance services tells none of them they are in the right place. Captive and independent agents also play by different rules. Captive agents usually work inside the carrier's brand rules, and some carriers manage the website for them, so the work starts with what you are allowed to change.

Two spots on one page

The person shopping a renewal, or setting up a new business, reads the sites under it and calls the agency that explained things plainly. An agency that holds a spot in both gets both calls.

Where the calls go missing

Six things we find on most insurance agency profiles

Each one is easy to miss. Together they send the quote request to the agency across town.

  1. Duplicate listings from the carrierCarriers and listing services sometimes create profiles for agents, and old ones linger after a move or a switch. Two listings for one office split the reviews and leave Google unsure which one is real.
  2. An old name that never left the directoriesAgents who go independent, or buy a book from a retiring agent, often leave the old name and phone number out there for years. The mismatch drags the profile down.
  3. One page for every lineHome, auto, life, and business all on a single page. Google picks one page to show for a search, and a page about every policy gives it no reason to pick yours for any of them.
  4. Reviews kept on a private widgetSome review tools collect feedback on a page Google never sees. Reviews on your Google profile are the ones that help on the map, so that is where the ask should point.
  5. No people on the profileInsurance is bought from a person. Stock photos and no faces give the shopper nothing to go on. Real photos of the office and the team make the call feel safe.
  6. Showing up for lines you do not writeAn agency that only writes personal lines and ranks for business insurance wastes the caller's time and yours. The categories and pages should match the policies you can actually quote.

How we work with insurance agencies

Built around how people find an insurance agent

01

We set up the categories and the profile

Insurance agency as the primary for most offices, or Insurance broker if that is how you work. Auto insurance agency, Home insurance agency, Life insurance agency, or Health insurance agency as secondaries only for lines you really write. Your hours say when a person will answer.

02

We build a page for each line you write

Home, auto, renters, umbrella, life, Medicare, and commercial lines like general liability or workers comp, only for what you quote. Each one gets its own page. Those pages are what put you in the results under the map.

03

We build a page for each town you write in

People like an agent they could meet in person, even if they never do. A page for each town you serve, with what people there tend to need, reaches them before they call anyone else.

04

We work inside your carrier's rules

If you are captive, we read the brand rules and check what the carrier already manages before we touch anything. If you are independent, we make it clear which carriers you can quote, with nothing on the page a carrier would object to.

05

We keep reviews coming

A text with a link goes out after a policy is bound or a claim is handled well. Replies thank the person and never mention their policy, their claim, or their health.

06

We report calls and quote requests

Each month you see where you rank across your towns, how that moved, and how many people called or asked for a quote. Traffic stays off the report because nobody buys a policy from traffic.

Questions

What insurance agencies ask us

We are a captive agent. Can we still do local SEO?

Usually, yes. What you can change depends on your carrier. Some manage the website or the listing for you, and some leave more of it to the agent. We start from your brand guide and agreement, and anything unclear goes to the carrier before we change it.

How long until the phone rings more?

Usually three to six months to start seeing big results. Fixing the profile and listings moves the map first. Line pages and town pages take longer and keep building.

We just went independent. What happens to our old listings?

We find the old profile and every directory listing with the former name or number, then update or merge them carefully. Some of it may belong to the carrier, and we tell you what can move with you before anything changes.

Can you help us win more commercial business?

Partly. The categories and pages decide which searches you show up for, so we build more around the commercial lines you want. Personal lines calls still come.

Can we list the carriers we write with?

Usually yes, as long as your appointments are current and the carrier allows it. A page that says who you can quote helps people who already have a carrier in mind.

We serve clients by phone across the state. Do we still need town pages?

The map still weighs distance from your office, so nearby towns come first. For clients further out, pages for each line of business do more of the work, along with reviews from people across the state.

Do you write about coverage on our site?

We write in plain words about what each policy is for and when people usually need it. You approve anything about coverage before it publishes, and nothing goes up that your carriers' rules do not support.
