Why Seattle is different

A city split up by water

Seattle sits between Puget Sound and Lake Washington, and the Ship Canal cuts across the city from Ballard to Lake Union. A trip from Ballard to Beacon Hill can mean a bridge, a hill, and a lot of traffic. People know that, so they look for someone close and on the same side of the water.

Two miles on the map can be a long drive here. People search like they know it.

The neighborhoods carry their own names and their own loyalty. Someone in Fremont types Fremont, and a family in West Seattle types West Seattle. A page that only says Seattle gives either of them little reason to think you work on their street.

Across Lake Washington, the Eastside is its own market. Bellevue, Redmond, and Kirkland have their own downtowns, and getting across the lake means one of a few busy bridges. A business in Ballard rarely shows on the map for a homeowner in Bellevue, however strong the profile. South of the city, Renton and Burien work the same way.

Two spots on one page

The map gets the call. The listing under it closes the deal.

When someone in Seattle searches for what you do, Google shows the map first and the regular results under it, and both change with the neighborhood they are searching from. A business that holds a spot in both, for that neighborhood, gets the call.

We work both. Your Google Business Profile wins the map. Your website wins the listing under it. The audit checks where you stand in each.

Questions

What Seattle owners ask us

You are not in Seattle. Does that matter?

No. Google looks at your location, your profile, your reviews, and your site, and none of those depend on where we work. We run Seattle campaigns remotely and report to you every month.

How long until I see results?

Usually three to six months to start seeing big results. Some neighborhoods move faster than others, and we tell you which before you commit.

We are in Bellevue. Can we show up for customers in Seattle?

In the results under the map, yes, with good pages for the Seattle neighborhoods you serve. On the map, distance counts, and the lake adds to it, so the Eastside comes first. A second listing in Seattle needs a real staffed office.

Can one profile cover the whole metro?

No single listing reaches from Shoreline to Renton and over to Issaquah. You win the area around your address first, then reach further with neighborhood and city pages as the profile gets stronger. A virtual office or a mailbox can get the whole profile suspended.

Every address here says Seattle. Do neighborhood pages still help?

Yes. Many people search with the neighborhood name when they want someone close. Your profile keeps your real address, and your pages name the neighborhoods the way locals say them.
