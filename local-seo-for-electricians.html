Why electrical work is different

Customers cannot see the work, so they check the electrician

Most people cannot judge wiring. It sits behind the walls, and if it is done badly they may not find out for years. So they judge the person instead. They read reviews for whether the electrician showed up on time, explained the problem, cleaned up, and charged what they said. They look for a license number.

The job list has also changed. Car chargers, panel upgrades, standby generators, batteries, and smart home wiring each bring their own searches, and the people typing them are usually planning a real project. Many local shops still have one page that lists all of it. A page for each job is how a shop wins those searches.

A homeowner who trusts their electrician calls them for every job in the house after that.

Then there is the split between homes and businesses. A property manager looking for a commercial electrician searches differently from a homeowner with a flickering light, and wants different proof. If you do both, each side needs its own pages.

Where the calls go missing

Six things we find on most electrician profiles

None of them look serious alone. Together they send calls to the shop down the road.

  1. Categories that miss the workElectrician alone, when the shop installs chargers and generators every week. Or extra categories for work nobody on the crew does. Electrician is the primary. Every other category matches real jobs.
  2. One page called ServicesPanels, chargers, generators, lighting, rewiring, and troubleshooting on one page. Google ranks whole pages, so a page about everything ranks for nothing.
  3. No license number anywhereCareful customers look for it before they let anyone open a panel. A site and profile with no license number make them wonder why.
  4. Hours that promise more than the phone deliversThe listing says open all night and the call goes to voicemail. The next review says so, and the next customer reads it.
  5. Homes and businesses blurred togetherOne page tries to speak to the homeowner and the facilities manager at once. Neither one finds what they came for.
  6. Stock photos of light bulbsCustomers want to see your panels, your trucks, and your crew. Google uses real photos to understand the business, and a stock image tells it nothing.

Questions

What electricians ask us

Should we list ourselves as open all night if we only take some emergency calls?

List what is true. If someone answers after hours, show it and build a page for emergency work. If night calls go to voicemail, keep the hours honest, because a listing that says open and a phone that rings out turns into bad reviews.

We do homes and businesses. One profile or two?

One business gets one profile. Each side gets its own pages on the site. A second profile only makes sense for a separate business with its own staffed office.

How long until we see more calls?

Usually three to six months to start seeing big results. Fixing the profile and listings moves the map first. Service pages and reviews take longer and keep building.

Can we rank for car chargers and generators if we are newer to that work?

Yes, if you do the work. A category, a page, and photos of real installs tell Google you do it. The customers who type those searches are planning a project, and the page is what they read before they call.

Do you put our prices on the site?

Only if you want them there. Some shops post a starting price for common jobs, and some explain what drives the cost. Both answer what people are typing, and you decide.

Most of our work comes from builders. Is this worth it?

It depends on what you want next. If you want more service calls and homeowner projects, yes, that is exactly what the map and the listings bring. If you plan to stay with builders, a strong profile still helps, but the push may not pay back.

Can we compete with the big franchise shops?

On the map, yes. A franchise location is one profile like yours, and Google ranks the closest, best reviewed, best described shop. You win your own towns and the project searches they do not cover well.
