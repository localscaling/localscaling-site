Why family law is different

People search for a family lawyer in private

Most people hire a family lawyer at the worst moment of their life. They may be searching on a phone their spouse can see, late at night, and they read carefully before they call anyone. They look for a firm that sounds calm, explains the process plainly, and talks about the children before it talks about winning.

Each kind of case is its own search. Divorce, custody, child support, adoption, protective orders, and prenups are typed by different people with different fears. A single page called practice areas tells none of them they are in the right place.

The person searching for a family lawyer wants to feel safe calling. Every page should make that easy.

The work is local by nature. Cases are filed in a county court, and people often search by county or ask for a lawyer who knows their judges. The rules are tight too. State bars limit how lawyers advertise, and reviews and replies have to protect the people involved. We write inside those rules, and an attorney at your firm approves every page.

Where the calls go missing

Six things we find on most family law profiles

Each one looks minor. Together they send the call to the firm across town.

  1. Lawyer as the only categoryGoogle has Family law attorney and Divorce lawyer as categories. A firm listed only as Lawyer tells Google less than the firms above it.
  2. One page for every kind of caseDivorce, custody, support, and adoption on a single page. Google ranks whole pages, so a page about everything ranks for nothing.
  3. Review replies that confirm a clientA warm reply that mentions a divorce or a custody fight tells the world that person was your client. Short replies with no details protect them and read better to the next person.
  4. Pages that sound like a fightAggressive and ruthless read badly to a parent who wants this over without hurting the kids. Plenty of people are looking for a steady hand.
  5. No word about cost or processPeople want to know how a retainer works and what happens first. A site that says call us for everything loses the reader who is not ready to call.
  6. Old names on the directoriesPartners come and go, and the old firm name and number keep living on the legal directories Google checks. The mismatch drags the profile down.

Questions

What family law firms ask us

Our clients do not want to leave reviews. How do we get any?

Many will not, and that is fine. We ask only clients whose matter went well and who are comfortable, and we never ask for details. A steady trickle of recent reviews does more than a big batch.

How long until the phone rings more?

Usually three to six months to start seeing big results. Fixing the profile and listings moves the map first. Case pages and county pages take longer and keep building.

Can you get us more of one kind of case?

Partly. The pages and categories decide which searches you win, so we build more around custody or high asset divorce if those are the cases you want. Other calls still come.

Should each attorney have a Google profile?

Sometimes. Google allows one for a lawyer who meets clients, with rules for how it is named. Set up right it can help. Set up wrong it competes with the firm. We decide that with you.

We practice in three counties but have one office. Can we rank in all three?

With a page for each county, often yes, and the profile gets stronger around your office first. A second profile needs a real staffed office. A virtual office or a mailbox can get the first one suspended.

Do you put our fees on the site?

Only if you want them there. Some firms explain how a retainer works and what drives the cost, and some list a starting figure for an uncontested divorce. Both answer what people are typing, and you decide.

Do you write about the law on our site?

We write the case pages and county pages in plain words. An attorney at your firm approves every page before it publishes, and nothing about outcomes, timelines, or fees goes up unless you have confirmed it.
