Why physical therapy is different

Patients choose the clinic, even with a referral

A doctor's referral used to settle where a patient went. Now most patients look up the clinic first. They check the map for distance, the reviews for whether the therapists listened, and the site for their insurance. A referral to a clinic with a thin profile often ends up at the clinic down the street.

Plenty of patients never see a doctor first. Every state lets people see a physical therapist without a referral in some form, with limits that vary by state, and some insurance plans still ask for one. People search for the problem they have, such as a frozen shoulder or a sore back, and the clinic with a clear page about it gets the call.

A patient comes two or three times a week for weeks. The clinic that is easy to reach wins that schedule.

Distance matters more here than in most care. Nobody wants a long drive three times a week, so patients pick a clinic near home or work. That makes the map, and the neighborhoods your profile reaches, most of the game.

Two spots on one page

The map gets the call. The listing under it closes the deal.

When someone searches for physical therapy, Google shows the map first and the regular results under it. The patient with a referral calls the closest clinic that looks good on the map. The patient comparing options reads the sites under it, checks which clinics take their insurance and treat their problem, and books with the one that answered both. A clinic that holds a spot in both gets both patients.

Where the calls go missing

Six things we find on most physical therapy profiles

None of them look serious alone. Together they send referrals and new patients to the clinic down the road.

  1. Categories that undersell the clinicPhysical therapy clinic or Physical therapist as the primary, then Sports medicine clinic or Occupational therapist only if that care is really offered. Many clinics stop at one category and leave the rest to competitors.
  2. One page called ServicesPost surgery rehab, sports injuries, back pain, vertigo, and pelvic floor therapy on one page. Google ranks whole pages, so a page about everything ranks for nothing.
  3. No word about insurancePatients want to know if you are in network before they call. A site that lists the plans you take, and says what happens if yours is not on the list, keeps them from calling someone else.
  4. Each location muddled with the othersClinic groups often share one phone number and one page for every location. Google cannot tell the clinics apart, so each one ranks worse than it should.
  5. Old details on the directoriesA clinic that moved, was bought by a group, or changed its name keeps the old details on health directories for years. The mismatch drags the profile down.
  6. Review replies that say too muchA reply that mentions someone's surgery or injury confirms they are a patient. Short, kind replies with no details are safer and read better to the next person.

Questions

What physical therapy clinics ask us

Most of our patients come from doctor referrals. Do we still need this?

Yes. Referred patients look you up before they call, and many pick a different clinic if yours looks thin. Doctors' staff check too. A strong profile and clear pages help the referral turn into a booked visit.

How long until we see new patients?

Usually three to six months to start seeing big results. Fixing the profile and listings moves the map first. Service pages and reviews take longer and keep building.

We have several locations. One profile or several?

One profile for each real location with staff and posted hours, each with its own phone number and its own page on your site. That way each clinic can rank in its own neighborhood.

Should each therapist have a Google profile?

Sometimes. Google allows one for a practitioner who sees patients, and it has rules for how it is named. Set up right it can help. Set up wrong it competes with the clinic. We decide that with you.

Can we get more of one kind of patient?

Partly. The pages and categories decide which searches you win, so we build more around pelvic floor or sports rehab if that is the work you want. Other patients still come.

We are out of network with most plans. Does that change anything?

A little. Patients still search the same way, but the questions change. Your pages explain how payment works and why you work that way, so the people who call are fine with it.

Do you write about health on our site?

We write service pages and city pages in plain words. You approve anything clinical before it publishes, and we make no claims about outcomes.
