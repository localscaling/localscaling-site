Why pest control is different

Half your calls are urgent and half are a yearly decision

A wasp nest by the front door or droppings in the pantry is an urgent call. People search on a phone, look at the map, and call the first company that looks real and answers. They rarely scroll far.

The rest of the business is slower. A homeowner signing up for a quarterly plan, a landlord who needs a building treated every month, or a family selling a house compares a few companies before choosing one. They read the pages, check the reviews for the technician's name, and look for a company that explains what it sprays and when.

One urgent call can turn into years of plan renewals. The map is where most of them start.

Each pest is its own search. Termites, bed bugs, mice, mosquitoes, and stinging insects are typed by different people at different times of year. A single page called services tells none of them they are in the right place. Pest control is licensed work too, and people want to know a licensed technician is coming into their home.

Where the calls go missing

Six things we find on most pest control profiles

Each one looks small. Together they send the call, and the yearly plan after it, to the truck down the street.

  1. A service area that covers half the stateDrawing the service area wider than your routes does not stretch the map. Google still weighs distance, and the area reads better when it matches the towns you really service.
  2. One page for every pestTermites, bed bugs, rodents, and mosquitoes on a single page. Google ranks whole pages, so a page about everything ranks for nothing.
  3. No license number anywhereYour state license is a real trust signal for a company that sprays inside people's homes. Many sites leave it off.
  4. Review replies that name the problemA reply that thanks someone for letting you treat their bed bugs tells their neighbors what they had. Short, warm replies with no details protect the customer and read better.
  5. Photos from a stock libraryA generic bug photo tells nobody anything. Real trucks, real technicians in uniform, and real work give Google and the customer something to trust.
  6. Old details from a buyoutPest control companies change hands often. The old name and phone number keep living on directories for years, and the mismatch drags the profile down.

Pest control service as the primary. Bird control service or Animal control service as secondaries only when you really do that work. Termites, bed bugs, mosquitoes, and rodents go in as services, and your hours say when someone will actually pick up.

02

We build a page for each pest and service

Termite inspections and treatment, bed bugs, mice and rats, ants, wasps and hornets, mosquito yard treatment, and recurring home plans, only for the work you do. Each one gets its own page. Those pages are what put you in the results under the map.

03

We build a page for each town on your routes

People want the company whose truck is already in their neighborhood. A page for each town you service, with the pests that show up there, reaches them before they call anyone else.

04

We answer the questions people ask before they call

Whether a treatment is safe around kids and pets, how long until they can go back in a room, what a plan includes, and how follow-up visits work. Only what is true for your products and your company, approved by you.

05

We keep reviews coming

The technician asks at the end of a visit that went well, and a text follows with a link. Recurring customers get asked once, after a few quiet months. Replies never name the pest or the address.

06

We report calls

Each month you see where you rank across your routes, how that moved, and how many people called. Traffic stays off the report because nobody signs a plan from traffic.

Questions

What pest control companies ask us

Most of our money is in recurring plans. Does local SEO help with that?

Yes. Most plan customers start with one urgent call or one search for a yearly service. A page for your plans, and reviews that mention the same technician coming back, help those calls turn into renewals.

How long until the phone rings more?

Usually three to six months to start seeing big results. Fixing the profile and listings moves the map first. Pest pages and town pages take longer and keep building.

We work out of a home office. Can we still rank on the map?

Yes. You hide the address and set a service area of the towns you really cover. Google still uses your real location to decide how far you reach, so town pages and reviews do the rest.

Can you get us more termite or bed bug work?

Partly. The pages and categories decide which searches you win, so we build more around the jobs you want. Other calls still come.

Should we list our prices?

Only if you want to. Some companies list a starting price for a one time treatment or a plan, and some explain what drives the cost. Both answer what people are typing, and you decide.

We just bought another company. What happens to its listings?

We look at both before anything changes. Usually the old profile and listings are merged or updated to the new name and number, carefully, so the reviews and history are not thrown away.

Do you write about chemicals and safety on our site?

We write in plain words about how you treat and what customers should do before and after. You approve anything about products or safety before it publishes, and nothing goes up that your labels and license do not support.
